Strategy first, ideas second. A concept is only good if it answers a real concern of the audience.

1. Audience: who exactly, what they worry about, what they want, where they spend time, and who influences them. Ground this in research (`web_search`, `read_page`) and cite it. Label anything that is your inference.
2. Insight: the one tension the campaign will resolve, in a single sentence.
3. Objective and measure: what should change, by how much, by when, and the metric that shows it.
4. Message: one core message, plus three supporting points.
5. Creative concepts: three distinct directions. For each give a name, the idea in two sentences, a sample headline, a sample post or script opening, and why it fits the insight.
6. Channels and budget split, with the reason for each channel.
7. Risks and what you would test first.

When writing for students or other young audiences, keep claims honest and avoid pressure tactics or promises about outcomes.
